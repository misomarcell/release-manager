Release Manager, refreshed

A proposal for the four feature pages on phone and desktop. It stays inside Material 3, replaces the stock azure-on-grey look with its own palette and type, and gives every page the same three building blocks: one title row, one detail view, one status strip. All titles, posters and release names shown here are invented placeholders.

Releases

Filters move out of the hidden menu into chips. A row answers three questions before it is opened: what is it, do I own it, and how good and how healthy are the releases. Opening a title is a page on the phone and a side pane on desktop, so the list never jumps.

Library

The same row and the same detail view, now fed by Jellyfin. Quality tags use one neutral style with two emphases (resolution, HDR) in place of five unrelated hues.

Watchlist

Amber means "new since you last looked" everywhere: the navigation badge, the row and the status strip. Nothing else in the interface uses it except rating stars.

Discover

Recommendations under a shorter name that fits a bottom bar, and the home of the new all-titles search. Releases and Library keep their own scoped search; this one reaches every title through TMDB, which is what the watchlist needs.

Foundations

What changes underneath. Swatches follow the theme switch at the top of the page.

Shape

Lists are segmented groups: large outer corners, 2px seams, and the selected item rounds fully. The status strip, release lists and sheets all reuse it.

What moves

  • Navigation becomes a bottom bar on phones and a rail on desktop; the drawer goes away.
  • Admin pages (users, administration, logs) and sign out move behind the account avatar.
  • Dialogs for download and watchlist become bottom sheets on phones.
